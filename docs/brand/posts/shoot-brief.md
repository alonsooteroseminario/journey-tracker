# Shoot brief

What to record, and how, for the footage-based reels. Decided 2026-09-04: the
account **stays faceless**, but takes the structure of the format that did 1.8M
plays (`reference-teardown.md` round 2). That means hands and screen, one
continuous shot, overlays carrying the motion.

Read this once. It is written to be followed with a phone and no crew.

## The three assets

| # | Asset | Used by | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Screen recording of the wallet | posts 07 to 09, and every later demo | highest |
| B | B-roll of you working, hands only | post 04 onward, the reel bed | high |

A is the one that unblocks the most. It is also the only one that cannot be

## A. Screen recording of the wallet

**60 to 90 seconds. No narration. Do not rush.**

Record the real thing at `buildcadence.co/wallet`, signed in, with real content
already in it. An empty wallet is the single most common way a product demo
reads as a prototype.

Before recording, seed it so it looks lived in:
- three or four wallets, named for real projects, not "Test" or "Demo 1"
- one wallet with at least three groups
- one group with four or more chunks, of visibly different lengths

Then record this sequence, pausing about a second between steps so there is
something to cut on:

1. The wallet list, sitting still for two seconds before anything moves.
2. Open a wallet. Let the three panes settle.
3. Open a group, so the chunks are visible.
4. Click into a chunk and edit a few words. Real typing, real speed.
5. Open the compose drawer and select three chunks.
6. Let the merged preview render. **Hold on this for four seconds.** This is the
   payoff frame and it is the one that will be paused on.
7. Copy. Let the toast appear and finish.

**How:** desktop browser, not the phone layout. Full screen, browser chrome
hidden if you can. Highest resolution your recorder offers. Light mode reads
better against the cream carousels; dark mode reads better in reels. If it is
cheap to do both, do both.

**Do not:** move the mouse in circles, click faster than a person reads,
show a notification, show your email address anywhere, or narrate.

## B. B-roll, hands and keyboard only

**One continuous take, 60 to 90 seconds. The camera never moves and the shot
never cuts.**

The reference reel is a single locked-off shot for its whole runtime, and all
the motion comes from overlays. That is what makes it repeatable weekly.

**Framing:** your hands on the keyboard, the laptop, and some of the desk. Crop
above the shoulders so no face enters the frame at any point. A slightly high
three-quarter angle from the side reads better than straight on.

**Camera:** phone on a tripod, or propped against books. It must not drift.
Shoot **vertical** if you only shoot once. Landscape is fine only if you also
shoot a vertical take.

**Light:** face a window, or put a lamp behind the camera. Never a window behind
you. Daylight is enough and it is free.

**Action:** actually work for the whole take. Type, pause, scroll, think, retype.
Do not perform. The reference works because nothing in it looks staged.

**Do not:** cut, zoom, pan, add music, add a filter, or let a face, a name, a
badge, or a screen with private content into frame.

**Shoot two takes.** The second one is always calmer than the first.

## C. Face photo

One clean photo, shoulders up, plain background, natural light. For the profile
picture only. It never appears in a post, which is what faceless means here.
